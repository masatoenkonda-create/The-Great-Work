# Procurement evidence for the layer discussion

Codex, 22 October 2026. Research for a brainstorm, not an approved implementation plan. All dates below are **date read: 22 October 2026**. “Read” means the named passages, not every page on a site or an entire publication. Web pages are recorded by URL and section; two original PDFs and two rendered page previews are retained here.

## Records actually read

### P01 — CIPS: Procurement and Supply Cycle — **Read**

[Original](https://1prd-dxp.cips.org/intelligence-hub/procurement/procurement-supply-cycle). Read stages 1–2, 5–8 and 10: specification, make/buy, requirements, supplier screening, quotations, evaluation and receipt. Supports a process beginning before order placement. Supplier capability and product suitability matter alongside price. **Limit:** professional framework, not a mandatory sequence of thirteen playable tasks or evidence for semiconductor prices.

### P02 — CIPS: Supplier evaluation — **Read**

[Original](https://1prd-dxp.cips.org/intelligence-hub/managing-suppliers/supplier-evaluation). Read “How do you evaluate a supplier?” and the three appraisal methods. Data sheets/test reports, visits and outside assessments provide different evidence. **Limit:** a sample test is one piece of evidence, not proof of future reliability or a real factory audit compressed into a button.

### P03 — APQC: purchasing process — **Read**

[Original](https://www.apqc.org/What-are-the-key-steps-involved-in-the-purchasing-process). Read steps 1–7 and FAQs distinguishing requisition, purchase order and wider sourcing. Order reconciliation compares received items/quantities with the order and resolves discrepancies. Payment continues in the broader procure-to-pay process. **Limit:** organizational process reference; our solo player combines several real roles and will not fill out forms.

### P04 — CIPS: Total cost of ownership — **Read**

[Original](https://1prd-dxp.cips.org/intelligence-hub/finance/total-cost-of-ownership). Read both definition sections and cost categories. Purchase, acquisition, use and disposal costs differ; lower unit prices at larger minimum quantities may bring excess stock and obsolescence exposure. **Limit:** no cost-share estimates for our TV. A teaching ledger covering selected manufacturing costs is not a complete TCO calculation.

### P05 — DigiKey: How to read a quote — **Read**

[Original](https://www.digikey.com/en/help-support/place-an-order/how-to-read-a-digi-key-quote). Read terminology guide, especially Pack Type, Minimum Release, Package Quantity, availability and quote validity. Manufacturer packaging and a quoted minimum are different; cut tape and custom reels exist. **Limit:** no universal minimum or claim that every component requires a full reel. No live quote or current price obtained.

### P06 — MicroChemicals: General information — **Read**

[Original](https://www.microchemicals.com/PRODUCTS/General-Information/). Read “Storage and handling of photoresists and ancillaries,” expiry meaning, storage temperature, particle formation and discolouration. Storage affects processing; ageing is gradual, and colour alone is not a performance test. **Limit:** applies to identified resist families, not a calibrated modern fab yield model. Does not justify instant failure at midnight on an expiry date.

### P07 — Kayaku Advanced Materials: Shelf Life Policy — **Read**

[Original](https://kayakuam.com/shelf-life-policy). Read product/partner shelf-life provisions and extension inquiry requirements. Remaining life at shipment and lot identity are actual commercial concerns; policies differ by product and supplier. **Limit:** no universal lifetime for “photoresist.” Our game must establish a material-specific acceptance window, not copy a shelf-life number onto every bottle.

### P08 — AIM Solder: Solder Paste Powder: When to Downsize — **Read**

[Original](https://www.aimsolder.com/wp-content/uploads/AIMTechnicalPaper_SolderPastePowderWhentoDownsize.pdf), [saved PDF](P08-aim-solder-powder-2024.pdf). Read pp. 1–3; rendered and visually inspected [p. 2](previews/P08-page-2.png). Powder/stencil matching has trade-offs; finer powder can increase price and processing difficulty. **Limit:** supplier technical paper; its screening heuristic is not a universal pass/fail law. The p. 1 conversion “190 µm/.004 inches” is internally inconsistent; do not reuse that conversion. Our stencil dimensions, paste and demonstration must be selected together.

### P09 — Dal Martello et al.: Pellets and lumps in silicon production — **Read**

[Original article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11663-011-9529-y), DOI 10.1007/s11663-011-9529-y (2011). Read abstract and Introduction paragraphs on quartz specifications and charge permeability. Chemistry, lump size and strength are separate requirements; excessive fines can obstruct gas permeability. **Limit:** laboratory work and solar-silicon context; not a recipe for our industrial furnace or a quartz-price-to-electrical-die-failure equation. HTML passages read; PDF not downloaded.

### P10 — Elkem: NTE power purchase agreement — **Read**

[Original producer announcement, Norwegian](https://www.elkem.no/nyheter/elkem-signerer-langsiktig-kraftkjopsavtale-med-nte-i-norge/), 1 July 2025. Read announcement body: a 2028–2037 agreement supports Salten operations. **Limit:** establishes that industrial power can be procured through long contracts; not Thamshavn's tariff, a one-off consumer offer or proof that a fixed contract is always cheaper. No current electricity prices obtained.

### P11 — Texas Instruments: Supplier General Quality Guidelines, Rev. A — **Read**

[Original](https://www.ti.com/lit/ml/szzo019a/szzo019a.pdf), [saved PDF](P11-ti-supplier-quality-guidelines.pdf), 18 PDF pages. Read §18 (p. 10), §21 (p. 11), §22–23 (p. 12); also §11 and the start of §15 (p. 8). Rendered and inspected [p. 10](previews/P11-page-10.png). Covers material-change approval, lot/process/test traceability and controlled storage. **Limit:** TI-specific requirements; PPAP discussion is explicitly automotive-related. Do not turn supplier approval into a universal TV legal certificate.

### P12 — Fraunhofer IIS: Foundry Services — **Read**

[Original](https://www.iis.fraunhofer.de/en/ff/sse/ic-design/virtual-asic-foundry.html). Read “Prototyping and small volume IC Services” and its service table. MPW pools different customer designs to share fabrication costs; prototype, small-volume and dedicated services differ. **Limit:** evidence for shared access, not a claim that our chip fits a named service, that an MPW saves every upstream cost, or that the player can obtain a current quoted price.

### P13 — Wacker / SunPower supply agreement, 3 August 2006 — **Read**

[Original filed exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/867773/000119312506232572/dex101.htm). Read §§3–5 and Appendix A product specification, packaging, labels/delivery note and claim handling. Actual example connects an order, specified material, lot, delivery and inspection. **Limit:** historical solar polysilicon contract with redacted terms; not today's electronic-grade specification, current law, price benchmark or universal commercial practice.

### P14 — Heraeus Electronics: Gold Bonding Wires — **Read**

[Original](https://www.heraeus-electronics.com/en/products-and-solutions/bonding-wires/fine-bonding-wires/gold-fine-bonding-wires/). Read product overview, wire families and applications. Different wire grades target different processing/reliability needs; TVs appear among applications. **Limit:** does not identify a particular TV SoC package, validate substituting copper into our gold-wire setup, or define which wire meets our fictional package specification. Linked factsheets not opened.

### P15 — CIPS: Make or Buy — **Read**

[Original](https://1prd-dxp.cips.org/intelligence-hub/procurement/make-or-buy). Read three decision pillars and arguments for/against buying. Capital requirements, production capacity and supplier dependence are part of the decision. **Limit:** not grounds to let procurement skip the whole production campaign; workshop-service purchasing is our proposed abstraction.

### P16 — Texas Instruments: Anti-counterfeit — **Read**

[Original](https://www.ti.com/quality-reliability/quality/anti-counterfeit.html). Read full short article. Authorized channels and supplier traceability matter; outside-channel parts may be counterfeit or unreliable. **Limit:** applies to purchased TI parts; does not imply every independent seller is dishonest or that Malaysia's player-made chip should be replaced by a purchased one.

## Leads and unresolved research

These are **Lead** questions, not supporting evidence for a build:

- **R1 / Germany:** current electronic-grade HCl/hydrogen supplier specifications, cylinder/plant-service terms, and actual shared-batch refining offers. Do not implement arbitrary “cheap gas gives dirty rods.”
- **R1 / Japan:** a specific semiconductor crucible's dimensions, cleanliness, fit and qualification evidence. No crucible supplier document was opened in this pass. Use this as a candidate interaction only.
- **R1 / Malaysia:** exact qualified wire/substrate/resin set and a demonstrated sample-test method. P14 establishes product variety, not our compatibility rules.
- **R2:** consistent cost boundaries and comparable production volumes for a silicon-to-TV cost breakdown. No defensible country cost percentages, solo-TV price or retail comparison established.
- **R3:** actual per-batch energy use, startup/base load and tariffs for our six fictional workcells. P10 supports procurement of electricity, not six cost rates.
- **R4:** original evidence tying TV production interruptions specifically to the 2020–2023 chip shortage. Not investigated beyond search leads in this pass; no new narrative claim adopted.
- **R5:** exact pack sizes/prices for the selected quartz, gases, crucible, resist, wire and paste. P05 supports the distinction between quantities and packaging, not universal industrial lot sizes.
- **Layer 3/4:** named transport lanes, damage/return terms, applicable compliance regimes and evidence required for this TV. Not researched here.

### P23 — Microchip Technology, Form 10-K for fiscal 2021 (year to 31 March 2021) — **Read**

### P24 — BCG × SIA, "Strengthening the Global Semiconductor Supply Chain in an Uncertain Era", April 2021 — **Read**

[Original](https://www.semiconductors.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/BCG-x-SIA-Strengthening-the-Global-Semiconductor-Value-Chain-April-2021_1.pdf), [saved PDF](P24-bcg-sia-value-chain-2021.pdf), 53 pages. Read PDF p. 39 (Exhibit 19: more than 50 points where one region has ~65%+ share; materials examples photoresist, silicon wafers, packaging substrates, specialty gases), p. 40 (single points of failure; sidebar of historical disruptions: 1993 Sumitomo Chemical plant explosion hit 60% of epoxy resin supply; 1999 Taiwan earthquake; 2011 Japan earthquake affected 25% of silicon wafer production; 2020–21 package-substrate plant fires; 2021 Renesas fab fire), p. 41 (C4F6 etch gas: "once a manufacturing plant is calibrated to use C4F6, it cannot be substituted"; Japan over 90% of the photoresist *processing equipment* market, which is equipment, not the resist itself), p. 45 (Exhibit 21: fully self-sufficient regional supply chains estimated at $900–1,225 billion upfront and a 35–65% rise in chip prices). Poppler printed font-weight warnings; text extracted normally. **Limit:** industry-commissioned analysis with modelled estimates; regional, not company or item level.

### P25 — Dubois & Pedersen, "Why partners do not fit into purchasing portfolio models", IMP Conference, Oslo, 2001 — **Read**

[Original](https://impgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/wpallimport/files/papers/170.pdf), [saved PDF](P25-imp-portfolio-170.pdf), 14 pages. Read p. 2 (purchased materials often 50–80% of cost of goods sold, quoting Cammish and Keough 1991) and pp. 4–5 (Kraljic's two dimensions quoted from Kraljic 1983 p. 110; Figure 1 four quadrants: non-critical, leverage, bottleneck, strategic; Kraljic p. 114 quoted on dependence: look for alternative suppliers or materials when weak, spread volume and buy spot when strong). **Limit:** secondary account written to *critique* portfolio models (partnerships don't fit them); Kraljic's own article not opened (P17).
