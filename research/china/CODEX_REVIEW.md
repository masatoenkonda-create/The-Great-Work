## Corrections that matter visually

**A populated board needs more than one chip.** Start with a fabricated main PCB carrying copper traces, solder mask and pads. Show paste printing across representative component footprints, then placement of our real incoming package plus a short automatic population of supporting components. Through-hole connectors, the opposite side, programming and separate power-board manufacture are compressed supplied/automatic work. Do not have RAM, connectors and resistors materialize as the chip reflows. If the scene uses a partly populated board instead, explicitly call this a selected assembly pass and print only its relevant sites.

**Paste is not just glue.** A squeegee rolls solder paste across a registered stencil; lifting the stencil leaves discrete deposits on exposed pads. The paste contains solder particles and flux. The matching package underside balls meet those deposits with the orientation mark aligned. Camera/fiducial positioning is automatic here; do not repeat Taiwan's alignment puzzle or make surface tension rotate a wrongly oriented package into electrical correctness.

**Reflow has a return to solid.** Show preheat → reflow → cooling along the conveyor. In one fixed magnified side view, paste/balls wet the board pads, the package settles slightly, and the joints solidify before handling. Self-centering corrects a small demonstrated offset; it is not a rescue for a package placed on the wrong footprint. The silicon and black mold body do not melt. Choose compatible lead-free balls/paste as a game assumption; actual alloy and package qualification remain unspecified.

**Name the right failure mechanism.** Too little thermal exposure risks incomplete reflow/wetting. Head-in-pillow is specifically a failure to coalesce even when ball and paste have melted; it is not a synonym for cold solder. Excessive exposure can worsen warpage or exhaust flux. These are risks, not deterministic diagnoses from one temperature reading. Use one physical sentence in the guide and a predicted profile; no simulated broken production material is required.

**Inspection is not omniscience.** X-ray contrast can show balls, vias and fine bond wires, as C13 demonstrates. In this selected package, draw simplified attenuation-style silhouettes, not a normal color rendering behind a transparent lid. The X-ray source/detector belong inside an inspection enclosure; a visible scanning line is a UI cue. Keep image orientation tied to the package marker. Optional void-like features are not automatically failed joints; omit scoring void percentages. Do not invent a hidden bridge to make an accepted pause-only run fail. “INSPECTED” is a better lamp than “ALL JOINTS PERFECT.” Electrical operation is checked later.

## Chosen TV architecture and handling

Use a generic **LED-backlit LCD TV**, not an OLED or an emissive LED wall. The incoming panel is a complete display module: glass/cell, backlight and its timing electronics are supplied together. The arriving power board includes the required LED-driver function. A separate main board carries our chip, memory and other support components. Speakers, wiring, shields, thermal parts, stand and cover are station parts. This is a declared representative modular architecture, not the C17 model copied literally: C17 shows an integrated main/power-board alternative.

Support the panel face-down on a padded assembly fixture. Seat boards on mounting points, secure them, connect the panel-data ribbon, power/backlight harness and speakers, then install required thermal parts/shields and the cover. Cables need visible connectors and latch/seat motion. The logic chip does not directly power the backlight. Finish connectors before mains power. A fixture rotates the completed TV upright; no unsupported glass floating or main board sliding through the panel. Show a small camera/color sensor viewing test patterns and finish with a short tone and moving picture. No independent calibration puzzle, invented frequency certification or fake measured color-accuracy values.
